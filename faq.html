Booking & payments

How do I book a trip through Wander?

Wander is an independent editorial publication — we write guides, we don't sell trips. Our content is free to read, and we don't take commissions from hotels, airlines, or tour operators. When you're ready to book, we recommend going directly to the property or operator we've recommended in the guide. This usually gets you the best rate and keeps more money with the people on the ground.

If you'd like personalised help planning an itinerary, our contact page explains how to reach us.

What is your cancellation policy?

Because Wander doesn't process bookings, we don't have a cancellation policy of our own. Cancellation terms will always be set by your airline, hotel, or tour operator — check with them directly before you confirm anything.

As a general rule we advise travellers to purchase comprehensive travel insurance before every trip. It's the single most reliable safety net for last-minute changes, medical emergencies, or unexpected disruptions.

Visas & entry requirements

Do I need a visa for the destinations you cover?

Visa requirements depend entirely on your nationality and the destination. Each of our destination guides includes a "Practical details" section with visa information current at the time of writing, but regulations change — sometimes at short notice.

Before you travel, always verify requirements with the official embassy or consulate of your destination country, or through your own government's travel advisory service. Processing times can be weeks, so start early.

Planning your trip

When is the best time to visit a destination?

There is no single right answer — it depends on what you're after. Shoulder season (just before or after peak) often offers the best balance of decent weather, thinner crowds, and lower prices. High season guarantees the easiest logistics; low season can be genuinely magical but may mean some attractions are closed.

Every guide on Wander includes a dedicated "When to go" section that weighs climate, festivals, costs, and crowds for that specific place. Use those recommendations as your starting point, then tailor to your own priorities.

What should I pack for a long trip?

The short answer: less than you think. Most experienced travellers eventually converge on one principle — carry-on only if at all possible. A well-chosen capsule wardrobe in neutral tones, a lightweight merino layer, and one versatile pair of shoes will see you through most destinations and climates.

More specific packing advice appears in our destination guides where it's relevant — cold-weather layering for Norway, lightweight linens for Rajasthan, waterproofs for Patagonia. Look for the "What to pack" note in each guide's practical section.

How do your destination guides stay up to date?

Each guide carries a publication or last-reviewed date. We revisit our most popular guides at least once a year and update prices, entry requirements, and recommendations when things change materially. That said, the travel world moves fast — always double-check opening times, prices, and visa rules independently before you go.

If you spot something that's out of date, please let us know — reader tip-offs are invaluable.
